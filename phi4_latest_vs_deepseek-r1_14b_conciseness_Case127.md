The resilience threshold is determined by the capacity of local hardware to sustain operations without external data inputs, which is contingent on VRAM and compute power. Tokenized transaction barriers, such as pay-to-query mechanics, impose mathematical boundaries on data access, requiring sufficient local resources to maintain autonomy. Over a multi-year horizon, establishing absolute data sovereignty necessitates robust local hardware capable of supporting intensive computational tasks and large datasets, ensuring operational independence from centralized networks. The operational perimeter of a self-sustaining offline data fortress is defined by the ability to process and store data internally, mitigating the risk of external interference and ensuring intellectual autarky.
