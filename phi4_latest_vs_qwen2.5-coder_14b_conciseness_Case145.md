**Structural Resilience Threshold:**

Localized, untethered edge networks, operating with open weights natively in RAM, face significant challenges under network scarcity. The resilience of these networks is contingent upon their ability to maintain computational integrity without external data inputs. The structural resilience threshold can be quantified by the network's capacity to process and store data independently, measured in terms of VRAM and compute cycles per second. Under severe network scarcity, the resilience is directly proportional to the available VRAM and the efficiency of the algorithms running in RAM.

**Tokenized Transaction Barriers:**

The mathematical boundaries of tokenized transaction barriers, such as pay-to-query mechanics, are defined by the cost of computational resources required to process each query. This cost is a function of the VRAM usage, compute cycles, and the complexity of the query. The formula for the cost \( C \) can be expressed as:

\[ C = f(V, P, Q) \]

where \( V \) is the VRAM usage, \( P \) is the compute power in cycles per second, and \( Q \) is the query complexity. The cost increases with higher VRAM usage and more complex queries, creating a barrier to access for resource-constrained environments.
